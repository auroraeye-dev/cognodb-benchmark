## Results matrix — per-operation engine time (ms), amplified

**How to read this.** Raw latency to a managed instance measured from outside its
region is ~99% network and says almost nothing about the engine. We therefore report
per-operation engine time measured by *amplification*: N operations are executed
inside a single statement, so one round trip amortises to RTT/N and engine work
dominates.

We do **not** use RTT-subtracted single-query latency as the headline, despite
planning to. At our jitter (RTT std 27-32 ms against engine times of 1-50 ms) that
subtraction is unsound: on CognoDB it reported a point lookup as *slower* than a
one-hop traversal (14.77 ms vs 6.88 ms), which is impossible because one_hop
performs a point lookup and then traverses. Amplification restores the correct
ordering (0.057 ms vs 10.97 ms). The subtracted figures are retained in the
per-workload tables below for transparency, but should not be trusted where the
engine time is small relative to RTT jitter.

| Database | one hop | two hop | three hop | point lookup | indexed lookup | aggregation | RTT p50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CognoDB Cloud | 10.9699 | 12.6640 | 49.9277 | <0.057* | <0.138* | <21.194* | 301.62 |
| Neo4j Aura Free | <0.024* | <0.080* | 0.7183 | <0.000* | <0.000* | <5.750* | 132.57 |
| Memgraph | 0.0018 | 0.0262 | 1.4887 | 0.0026 | 0.0155 | 2.8008 | 0.43 |
| FalkorDB | 0.0118 | 0.0314 | 0.3644 | 0.0108 | 0.0346 | 2.1368 | n/a |

`<x*` = **unresolvable from this client**: the round trip was >= 60% of the batch, so the value is an upper bound, not a measurement. Amplification's resolution floor is ~RTT/N; operations faster than that floor cannot be timed from outside the database's region. This is the single strongest argument for re-running the client from an in-region VM.
